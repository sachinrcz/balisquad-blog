## How to Find Events in Bali (Without Living in WhatsApp Groups)

Bali has no shortage of things happening. Networking dinners, cowork sessions, sports nights, yoga circles, tech meetups, game nights — most weeks are packed.

The hard part isn’t “is there anything on?” It’s finding it before it already happened.

If you’ve ever heard about a great evening the morning after, or scrolled Instagram Stories hoping something useful appears, you’re not alone. Events here still travel mostly through private WhatsApp groups, Instagram posts that disappear in 24 hours, and “ask a friend who knows a friend.”

Here’s a clearer way to find what’s actually on — and show up.

---

### Why Bali events are so easy to miss

A lot of Bali’s best gatherings are small, local, and informal. That energy is part of what makes them good. It also means:

- They’re shared in closed WhatsApp groups you aren’t in yet
- Instagram is the main channel, but Stories vanish fast
- There’s no single “city calendar” everyone checks
- Newcomers especially have no idea where people actually meet

So you end up either over-networking to get into the right chats… or missing half of what’s happening near you.

That’s the gap BaliSquad was built for: one public place to see events across Bali, join them, and host your own — without needing insider access first.

---

### Still useful: Instagram, WhatsApp, and friends — with limits

BaliSquad doesn’t replace every channel. It just stops those channels from being your only option.

**Instagram** is still great for vibe checks — photos, reels, who’s hosting. Follow organizers and communities you like. Just don’t rely on Stories as your calendar.

**WhatsApp groups** are where a lot of last-minute plans land. Once you’ve been to a few events, you’ll naturally get invited into the right ones. Treat them as a bonus layer, not your starting point.

**Friends and coworkers** will always tip you off to hidden gems. Go to one good meetup and you’ll usually leave with two more recommendations.

Use those channels. Just don’t wait on them to discover that something exists.

---

### Match the event to what you actually want

Not every “meetup” is the same. Before you RSVP, ask what you’re looking for that week:

- **Easy social** — game nights, dinners, casual hangouts
- **Active** — badminton, padel, runs, beach sports
- **Focus + people** — coworking sessions, AI cowork nights
- **Learning** — workshops, lightning talks, skill meetups
- **Wellness** — yoga, circles, quieter connection

BaliSquad’s event pages usually make the vibe obvious. If something says “networking dinner” or “AI coworking,” you already know the tone. Start with one type that feels low-pressure, then branch out.

---

### A simple weekly rhythm that works

If you want a sustainable way to meet people (not a full-time event grind):

1. **Sunday or Monday** — open BaliSquad and star 2–3 things for the week
2. **Pick one easy win** — something free, nearby, and low commitment
3. **Show up early once** — introductions are easier before the room fills
4. **Talk to the host** — organizers remember faces and can point you to the next good thing
5. **Recap lightly** — if you liked it, follow that community or host for next time

Consistency beats FOMO. One solid event a week builds a real circle faster than five random ones.

---

### Tips if you just arrived in Bali

Landing week can feel lonely even when the island is buzzing. A few practical moves:

- Start with **open, free events** near where you’re staying
- Uluwatu / Bukit and Canggu both have dense calendars — don’t try to cover the whole island at once
- Coworking meetups are often the softest entry if you’re remote-working
- Sports and game nights are underrated for making friends fast
- Say yes to one thing before you feel “ready”

You don’t need a perfect network on day three. You need one room where people are happy you showed up.
