## A weekend in Ubud: a community itinerary

Ubud is where a lot of people come to slow down: rice fields behind the main road, gamelan drifting out of courtyards, and a steady mix of visitors, artists and remote workers who stick around longer than they planned. If you only have a few days, an **ubud weekend itinerary** works best when you anchor each day around one walk, one meal you actually sit down for, and one way to meet people — not a checklist of every temple on the island.

This plan spreads across **Friday evening, Saturday and Sunday**. Everything below is a real place we checked online as operating in late 2026. Hours and prices can change; confirm on the day. For rents, clinics and the bigger picture, start with our [Ubud area guide](https://balisquad.com/guides/ubud). If you are still choosing a base, the [where to stay in Bali for a month](https://balisquad.com/blog/p/where-to-stay-in-bali-for-a-month/#ubud) guide has an Ubud section with trade-offs and rough costs.

You will want wheels for some of these stops. Our [scooter rental guide](https://balisquad.com/blog/p/renting-a-scooter-in-bali/) covers licences, helmets and what riding feels like in the hills around Ubud.

## Before you go: keep the weekend flexible

Ubud runs on ceremonies, rain showers and the occasional one-way traffic tweak on Jalan Raya. Build in buffer time, especially if you are coming up from the coast on Friday.

- **Leave one evening open** for whatever is on [Ubud events on BaliSquad](https://balisquad.com/event/in/ubud) — dinners, circles, coworking socials and sports pop up every week.
- **If you need a desk between outings**, our [coworking in Ubud](https://balisquad.com/blog/p/coworking-in-ubud/) guide lists spaces and laptop-friendly cafés that are open now (and names that closed years ago so you do not waste a ride).
- **Dress for temples and palaces:** shoulders covered, sarong where required. Most sites lend or include a sarong with the ticket.

## Friday evening: arrive, wander central Ubud, catch a dance

You do not need a packed schedule on arrival night. The center is walkable once you are parked, and the best first impression is simply being in the lanes around the palace and market.

### Stroll the Ubud Art Market and palace crossroads

The art market sits opposite **Puri Saren Agung (Ubud Palace)** on Jalan Raya Ubud. Souvenir stalls usually open through the morning and into the late afternoon; exact times vary stall by stall, but planning for **roughly 10:00–17:00** for the craft section is a safe bet. Mornings are busier with produce; late afternoon can be quieter for browsing.

- [Ubud Art Market on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ubud%20Art%20Market%2C%20Jl.%20Raya%20Ubud%20No.35%2C%20Ubud%2C%20Bali)
- [Puri Saren Agung (Ubud Palace) on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Puri%20Saren%20Agung%2C%20Jl.%20Raya%20Ubud%20No.8%2C%20Ubud%2C%20Bali)

Daytime entry to the palace’s **public courtyards is free** (inner royal areas stay private). Published hours are often **08:00–18:00**, with some listings showing 07:00–18:00 — check at the gate if a ceremony is on.

### Early dinner at Clear Cafe

**Clear Cafe** is a long-running Ubud spot for fresh, mostly healthy plates — easy if half your group is vegetarian. The official site lists **daily 08:00–23:00** at Jalan Hanoman (Google Maps shows a 22:00 close some days, so do not cut it too late if you are heading to a show).

- [Clear Cafe on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Clear%20Cafe%2C%20Jl.%20Hanoman%20No.8%2C%20Ubud%2C%20Bali)

Eat around **18:00** if you want time to walk back toward the palace for the evening performance.

### Legong or Barong at Ubud Palace (19:30)

Most nights, the palace courtyard hosts a traditional dance with live gamelan. Schedules are published by local tourism desks and vary by day of the week — **Friday is often Barong**; other evenings may be Legong or Ramayana excerpts. Performances commonly start at **19:30** and run about **90 minutes**. Tickets are typically around **IDR 100,000**, sold on the day near the palace or through local ticket vendors — arrive **30–45 minutes early** if you want a good seat.

Double-check the dance title for your date when you buy a ticket; programs can shift for ceremonies.

## Saturday: ridge walk, farmers market, yoga, monkeys

Saturday is your “green Ubud” day: valley air early, then food and culture closer to town.

### Campuhan Ridge Walk (go early)

The **Campuhan Ridge Walk** is a free, paved path along a ridge west of central Ubud, starting near **Pura Gunung Lebah** at the Campuhan bridge. There is no gate or ticket — just show up in daylight. Most people allow **1.5–2 hours** return if you stop for photos; the path has **little shade**, so **06:00–07:30** is the sweet spot before the sun gets harsh. Head back before dark; the trail is not lit.

- [Campuhan Ridge Walk (Pura Gunung Lebah) on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Pura%20Gunung%20Lebah%20Campuhan%2C%20Ubud%2C%20Bali)

If you walk north toward Bangkiang Sidem, **Karsa Kafe** is a popular end point for breakfast above the rice fields. Listed hours are roughly **08:00–19:30** daily. Note that **Grab and Gojek pickups from inside Bangkiang Sidem village have been restricted** — fine for drop-off, awkward for ride-hail back; plan to walk return or arrange transport from outside the village ([Karsa Spa FAQ](https://karsaspa.com/faq/)).

- [Karsa Kafe on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Karsa%20Kafe%2C%20Jl.%20Bangkiang%20Sidem%2C%20Ubud%2C%20Bali)

### Ubud Organic Farmers Market (Saturday only)

From about **09:00 to 13:00 on Saturdays**, the **Ubud Organic Farmers Market** sets up in front of **Pizza Bagus** on Jalan Raya Pengosekan — organic produce, bread, jams and small makers. It is a easy place to stock fruit for the week and chat with growers. ([Ubud Organic Market](https://ubudorganicmarket.org/))

- [Pizza Bagus / Ubud Organic Farmers Market on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Pizza%20Bagus%2C%20Jl.%20Raya%20Pengosekan%2C%20Ubud%2C%20Bali)

### Drop-in class at The Yoga Barn

**The Yoga Barn** runs a large daily schedule of yoga, meditation and movement classes. The official daily-class page lists hours as **Sun–Fri 06:30–21:00** and **Sat 06:30–22:30**. Classes are **drop-in, first come first served**; registration opens **30 minutes before** daytime classes and **one hour before** evening classes.

Pick something gentle if you walked the ridge already — or save Yoga Barn for Sunday if you prefer a slower morning.

- [The Yoga Barn on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=The%20Yoga%20Barn%2C%20Jl.%20Sukma%20Kesuma%2C%20Peliatan%2C%20Ubud%2C%20Bali)

### Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary (late morning or mid-afternoon)

The **Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary** (Jalan Monkey Forest) is open **09:00–18:00** with **last entrance at 17:00**, per the official visit page. Foreign adult admission is **IDR 130,000** (child **IDR 100,000** on the same page). Secure bags, glasses and phones — the macaques are wild animals, not props.

Allow **1–2 hours**. Weekends get busy; late afternoon can be slightly calmer than midday tour groups.

- [Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Sacred%20Monkey%20Forest%20Sanctuary%2C%20Jl.%20Monkey%20Forest%2C%20Ubud%2C%20Bali)

### Optional: Tegallalang Rice Terrace (if you have a scooter and energy)

North of town, **Tegallalang Rice Terrace** is the classic stepped-paddy photo stop. Many recent listings give **07:00–18:00** daily hours and about **IDR 25,000** entry paid in cash at the gate — arrive early if you want fewer buses in frame. Paths can be slick after rain; wear shoes with grip.

- [Tegallalang Rice Terrace on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Tegallalang%20Rice%20Terrace%2C%20Tegallalang%2C%20Bali)

## Sunday: lotus pond, Elephant Cave, coffee and a soft landing

Sunday is lighter: one temple you can enjoy without rushing, one site outside the immediate center, and a café where you can debrief the weekend.

### Morning at Pura Taman Saraswati (Ubud Water Palace)

**Pura Taman Saraswati** is the lotus-pond temple behind the main drag on Jalan Kajeng. The **outer walkways and pond are free** during public visiting hours; the inner sanctum is for worshippers. Common published hours are **07:00–17:00** or **08:00–18:30** depending on the source — mornings are best for open lotus flowers and photos with fewer people.

Evening dance performances here are **separate ticketed events** on some nights; daytime visits are just a quiet stroll.

- [Pura Taman Saraswati on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Pura%20Taman%20Saraswati%2C%20Jl.%20Kajeng%2C%20Ubud%2C%20Bali)

### Goa Gajah (Elephant Cave)

**Goa Gajah** in Bedulu is a short ride from central Ubud — an archaeological site with a carved cave entrance, bathing pools and quiet paths. Gianyar regency tariff listings commonly cite **08:00–17:00** hours and **IDR 50,000** for foreign adults (**IDR 25,000** for foreign children), with sarong included at the ticket window. Cash helps for parking; confirm hours at the gate when you arrive.

- [Goa Gajah on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Goa%20Gajah%2C%20Bedulu%2C%20Gianyar%2C%20Bali)

### Seniman Coffee Studio (debrief cup)

**Seniman Coffee Studio** roasts on site and has been a Ubud meeting point for years — good Wi‑Fi, serious espresso, and room to linger over the weekend. Official hours: **daily 07:30–22:00** at Jalan Sriwedari.

If you are extending the trip and need a desk tomorrow, pair this stop with a day pass from the [coworking in Ubud](https://balisquad.com/blog/p/coworking-in-ubud/) list.

- [Seniman Coffee Studio on Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Seniman%20Coffee%20Studio%2C%20Jl.%20Sri%20Wedari%20No.5%2C%20Ubud%2C%20Bali)

### Sunday evening: one more public event (optional)

If you are not flying out until Monday, repeat the Friday pattern in reverse: check [Ubud events](https://balisquad.com/event/in/ubud), say yes to one low-pressure gathering, and let introductions happen naturally. That is how most of us find the WhatsApp groups and studio invites worth keeping — after we have already met people in real life.
